Every team has a rhythm — a set of recurring practices that keep work organized and everyone on the same page. Google is designed to support these rhythms without getting in the way. The workflows below represent the patterns used most often by high-performing teams, and each one can be adapted to fit your own process.

<Tabs>
  <Tab title="Sprint Planning">
    Sprint planning helps your team commit to a focused set of work for a fixed time period — typically one or two weeks. Use this workflow at the start of every sprint to align priorities and distribute work fairly.

    <Steps>
      <Step title="Create a Sprint">
        1. Open your project and navigate to the **Sprints** section in the left sidebar.
        2. Click **+ New Sprint** and enter a sprint name (for example, "Sprint 12 — May 6–19").
        3. Set the **Start Date** and **End Date** to define the sprint window.
        4. Click **Create Sprint** to save. The sprint appears as a dedicated section in your project.
      </Step>

      <Step title="Add Tasks to the Sprint">
        1. From your project backlog, select the tasks you want to include in this sprint.
        2. Right-click a task (or check the box and use the bulk action bar) and choose **Move to Sprint**, then select the sprint you just created.
        3. Alternatively, create new tasks directly inside the sprint section by clicking **+ Add Task** within the sprint row.
        4. Review the task list with your team to confirm scope before the sprint begins.
      </Step>

      <Step title="Assign Story Points">
        1. Open each task and click the **Points** field (displayed as a number badge next to the task title).
        2. Enter a point estimate that reflects the effort required — common scales include 1, 2, 3, 5, 8, and 13.
        3. Review the sprint's **Total Points** shown in the sprint header to make sure the workload is realistic for your team's capacity.
        4. Adjust task assignments or point values as needed, then mark the sprint as **Active** to begin.
      </Step>
    </Steps>
  </Tab>

  <Tab title="Daily Standup">
    Async standups remove the need for a daily meeting while still keeping everyone informed about blockers, progress, and plans. Use Google's activity feed and @mention features to run standups entirely in writing.

    <Steps>
      <Step title="Post Your Standup Update">
        1. Open your project's **Activity Feed** by clicking the **Activity** tab at the top of the project.
        2. Click **Add a comment** and write your standup note using this format:
           - **Yesterday:** What you completed.
           - **Today:** What you plan to work on.
           - **Blockers:** Anything preventing progress.
        3. Press **Post** to share the update with the entire project team.
      </Step>

      <Step title="Use @Mentions to Flag Blockers">
        1. In your standup comment or on a specific task, type `@` followed by a teammate's name to notify them directly.
        2. The mentioned teammate receives an in-app notification and, depending on their preferences, an email alert.
        3. Use @mentions to ask questions, request reviews, or escalate blockers without switching to another tool.
      </Step>

      <Step title="React and Acknowledge">
        1. Team members can react to standup comments with emoji reactions to acknowledge updates quickly.
        2. If a blocker needs discussion, reply directly in the thread to keep context attached to the project.
        3. Resolve blockers by updating the relevant task's status and posting a follow-up comment.
      </Step>
    </Steps>
  </Tab>

  <Tab title="Task Review">
    The task review workflow ensures work is checked for quality before it's marked complete. Use it whenever a task requires sign-off from a teammate, a manager, or a stakeholder.

    <Steps>
      <Step title="Move a Task to In Review">
        1. When you finish work on a task, open it and change the **Status** from *In Progress* to **In Review**.
        2. The task automatically appears in the *In Review* column on the Board view, signaling that it is ready for feedback.
        3. @mention the reviewer in a comment to notify them directly — for example, "@Alex, this is ready for your review."
      </Step>

      <Step title="Leave Comments and Feedback">
        1. The reviewer opens the task and reads the description, attached files, or linked pull requests.
        2. To leave feedback, click **Add a comment** and write specific, actionable notes.
        3. Use the **Checklist** feature inside the task to list individual review criteria and check them off as they are verified.
      </Step>

      <Step title="Request Changes or Approve">
        1. If changes are needed, the reviewer changes the task status back to **In Progress** and @mentions the assignee so they know to address the feedback.
        2. Once all feedback is addressed, the reviewer sets the status to **Done** (or **Approved**, if your workflow uses that status).
        3. The completed task moves to the Done column and contributes to the sprint's completed point total.
      </Step>
    </Steps>
  </Tab>

  <Tab title="Weekly Report">
    A weekly report gives stakeholders a clear view of what was accomplished, what slipped, and what is coming next. Generate one in Google without any manual data gathering.

    <Steps>
      <Step title="Open the Reports Section">
        1. Navigate to your project and click the **Reports** tab in the top navigation bar.
        2. Select **Completed Tasks Report** from the report type menu.
        3. Set the date range to cover the past seven days (or your custom week definition).
      </Step>

      <Step title="Review the Summary">
        1. The report displays all tasks completed within the selected period, grouped by assignee or label.
        2. Review the **Velocity** chart to compare this week's output against previous weeks.
        3. Note any tasks that were due but are still open — these appear in the **Overdue** section and may need to be discussed in your next planning session.
      </Step>

      <Step title="Export or Share the Report">
        1. Click **Export** to download the report as a CSV or PDF.
        2. Alternatively, click **Copy Shareable Link** to send a read-only view of the report directly to stakeholders — no login required.
        3. Schedule an automated delivery by clicking **Schedule Report**, choosing the frequency, and entering recipient email addresses.
      </Step>
    </Steps>
  </Tab>
</Tabs>
