Google is built around a clear, layered structure that keeps your team's work organized from the highest level down to the smallest detail. Whether you're managing a single project with a handful of collaborators or running a large organization with multiple teams working in parallel, understanding how the platform's core entities relate to one another helps you get the most out of every feature.

## The Building Blocks

Google organizes everything around four primary entities. Each one serves a distinct purpose, and together they form the complete picture of your team's work.

| Entity | What It Is |
|---|---|
| **Workspace** | The top-level container for your entire organization. All projects, members, and settings live here. |
| **Projects** | Organized groups of related work within a workspace. A project has its own timeline, settings, and members. |
| **Tasks** | Individual work items inside a project. Tasks carry all the detail: assignees, due dates, status, comments, and labels. |
| **Members** | Users who belong to a workspace, each assigned a role that determines what they can see and do. |

<Note>
Every piece of data in Google belongs to exactly one workspace. Projects belong to a workspace, tasks belong to a project, and members are granted access at the workspace level. This hierarchy keeps your data clean and your permissions predictable.
</Note>

## How It All Connects

A workspace is your organization's home base — everything starts there. Inside your workspace, you create projects to represent distinct bodies of work, such as a product launch, a client engagement, or an ongoing operational process. Each project contains tasks, which are the atomic units of work your team acts on every day.

Members join at the workspace level and can be assigned to one or more projects. Their workspace-level role sets a baseline for what they can access, while project-level settings let you fine-tune visibility and contribution rights. Comments, attachments, and activity history all live on tasks, making every work item a self-contained record of decisions and progress. This connected structure means nothing falls through the cracks — from a high-level roadmap view right down to a single to-do, everything is traceable.

## Your Workflow

The typical path from getting started to delivering results follows these steps:

<Steps>
  <Step title="Create Your Workspace">
    Set up your workspace with your organization's name and invite settings. This is your team's permanent home in Google.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Add Members">
    Invite teammates by email and assign each one a role — Owner, Admin, Member, or Viewer — so everyone has the right level of access from day one.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Create a Project">
    Add a project inside your workspace to represent a focused area of work. Give it a name, a description, and a status so your team knows what phase it's in.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Add Tasks">
    Break your project down into individual tasks. Set titles, descriptions, assignees, due dates, and labels to give each task full context.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Track Progress">
    Monitor task statuses as work moves from **To Do** through **In Progress**, **In Review**, and **Done**. Use project views and activity feeds to stay aligned as a team.
  </Step>
</Steps>
